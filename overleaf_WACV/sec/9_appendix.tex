\subsection{Fate of the missed animals}

\Cref{sec:analysis} reports that of the 83 animals in the held-out videos, 9 are never
detected, 27 are found but rejected by the confirmation rule, and 47 are covered by an
accepted track. \Cref{tab:missed} gives the per-group statistics behind that split: missed
animals are smaller and shorter-lived than counted ones, and the rejected group sits between
the other two on every measure --- which is precisely why a threshold on any single one of
them cannot separate it.

\begin{table}[t]
\centering\small
\begin{tabular}{lrrrr}
\toprule
Outcome & $n$ & Median box & GT frames & Cand.\ conf \\
\midrule
Counted & 47 & 38.1\,px & 102 & 0.76 \\
\textbf{Rejected by the rule} & \textbf{27} & 28.0\,px & 47 & 0.52 \\
Never detected & 9 & 19.9\,px & 47 & --- \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{\textbf{Fate of the 83 unseen animals.} The detector finds 74 of 83; the
confirmation rule then discards 27 of them. Missed animals are smaller and shorter-lived
than counted ones.}
\label{tab:missed}
\end{table}

\subsection{Held-out protocol}

Counting accuracy measured over all 32 videos, 19 of which trained the detector, is 89.0\%;
on the 13 videos the detector never saw it is 66.3\%.

\begin{table}[t]
\centering\small
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\toprule
Evaluation scope & Videos & Deer & Coverage \\
\midrule
Detector-training videos & 19 & 153 & 99.3\% \\
\textbf{Unseen (val + test)} & \textbf{13} & \textbf{83} & \textbf{66.3\%} \\
All 32 (the default) & 32 & 236 & 89.0\% \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{\textbf{Why the held-out protocol matters.} Evaluating on all 32 videos, as a
naive pipeline evaluation would, reports 89\% coverage. On video the detector has never
seen it is 66.3\%. We report the latter throughout.}
\label{tab:leak}
\end{table}

\subsection{Candidate pools}

\Cref{tab:pools} repeats the four candidate-generation configurations of
\cref{sec:inversion} with the ceiling column made explicit: \primaryq as a percentage of
the 83 held-out animals, which is the most a confirmation stage emitting one count per track
could reach on that pool.

\begin{table*}[t]
\centering\small
\begin{tabular}{llrccccc}
\toprule
Pool & Intervention & Candidates & \reachedq & \primaryq & \countedq & MAE & Ceiling \\
\midrule
C & Orphan recovery, conf 0.10 (operating point) & 7,008 & 74/83 & 66/83 & \textbf{55/83 = 66.3\%} & \textbf{2.38} & 79.5\% \\
E & \quad + track-initialization gate removed, conf 0.02 & 18,349 & 79/83 & 69/83 & 54/83 = 65.1\% & 2.54 & 83.1\% \\
F & \quad + NMS IoU $0.50\!\to\!0.90$ & 90,239 & 79/83 & 70/83 & 41/83 = 49.4\% & 3.46 & 84.3\% \\
G & \quad + appearance re-identification & 27,679 & \textbf{82/83 = 98.8\%} & \textbf{73/83 = 88.0\%} & 52/83 = 62.7\% & 2.69 & \textbf{88.0\%} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{\textbf{Every improvement to candidate generation degrades the count.} Pool G
reaches 82 of 83 unseen animals and gives 73 their own track --- the best pool we built, at
less than a third of pool F's candidate count --- yet counts fewer animals than pool C, which
reaches only 74. ``Ceiling'' is \primaryq/83, the maximum a one-count-per-track confirmation
stage could achieve. \reachedq and \primaryq are counted per animal; \countedq is the
per-video capped aggregate defined in \cref{sec:protocol}, which is the figure the
counting literature reports. Its per-animal equivalent is lower --- 47, 47, 37 and 47
respectively --- and is discussed below.}
\label{tab:pools}
