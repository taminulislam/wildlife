\abstract{Wildlife surveys often aim to estimate the abundance of animals in an area, yet
automated pipelines are usually judged as object detectors, by how well they locate animals
in individual frames rather than by how many distinct animals they count. We test whether
detection accuracy predicts counting accuracy on thermal road-transect video. We introduce 32
fully annotated FLIR thermal road transects containing 236 individually tracked white-tailed
deer, benchmark twelve detectors, and build TRACT, a counting pipeline whose error we
decompose into detection, association and confirmation. The confirmation rule is fitted on
training videos and frozen before evaluation on videos the detector never saw. Scored per
animal rather than per box, detection is close to solved. The reported pipeline finds 89.2\%
of held-out animals, gives 79.5\% a distinct track and counts 66.3\%; the richest candidate
pool we built reaches 98.8\% and counts fewer still, at 62.7\%. Every configuration that
enriches the candidate pool makes the final count worse, and learned confirmation heads all
lose to a three-parameter rule. In this survey, detection is not the bottleneck; confirmation
is. At this corpus size it is limited by supervision rather than model capacity, because the
extra candidates a better detector supplies are re-sightings of animals already counted.}

\keywords{Thermal imaging, Wildlife survey, Animal counting, Object detection, Multi-object tracking, White-tailed deer}
