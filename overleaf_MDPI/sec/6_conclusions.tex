\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusion}

TRACT counts white-tailed deer from thermal road transects, and decomposing its error shows
that the accuracy a practitioner would try to buy with a better detector is being lost
somewhere else: on held-out video the reported pipeline reaches $89.2\pm8.5\%$ of animals, gives
$79.5\pm9.9\%$ a distinct track and counts $66.3\pm10.7\%$, while four independent improvements
to the first two stages each made the third worse: the richest pool reaches $98.8\pm5.3\%$ and
counts only $62.7\pm10.7\%$. The cause is supervision
rather than model class. The pools offer roughly two hundred positive tracks, and duplicate
tracks on animals already counted sit \emph{between} the positives and the false candidates
in every feature we measured, so learned confirmers of three capacities lose to a
three-parameter rule whose confidence condition turns out to be performing duplicate
suppression rather than the recall filtering it resembles. Moving that threshold to where
signed bias crosses zero recovers 81 of the 83 held-out animals in aggregate
(\cref{sec:operating}), which makes the under-count an operating-point choice rather than a
limit of the system. We release the dataset, per-individual annotations, evaluation harness
and decomposition tooling: the gap between what is reachable and what is counted is large,
precisely localized and entirely open.
